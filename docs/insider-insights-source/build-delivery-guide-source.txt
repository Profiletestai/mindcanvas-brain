# 1. Purpose and Build Outcome

This guide explains how the four Insider Insights reports should be scored, selected, assembled, linked, rendered and used by the internal team. The four reports are not static documents that should be shown in full. They are master internal knowledge bases. The live output is created dynamically from the test taker’s assessment record and should contain only the content that is relevant to that person.

The purpose of the Insider Insights layer is different from the purpose of the client-facing Full Diagnostic. The client report creates recognition and direction for the founder. Insider Insights equips the coach, adviser or consultant to understand the founder more deeply, prepare for the conversation, communicate in a way that is likely to land, identify what to challenge, interpret buying behaviour, guide a clean decision and coach the rebuild after the sale.

| **CORE INTERNAL PRINCIPLE** Approach explains how the founder may operate. Pillar results show what is happening. Constraint logic tells you where to intervene. The book provides the commercial philosophy and coaching intervention. The live conversation validates the hypothesis. |
| --- |

The build must preserve that order. The internal report should never become a personality-led sales script. A Future-Led, Connection-Led, Timing-Led or Evidence-Led result is a behavioural lens. The six pillar results and Constraint Engine are the commercial evidence. Where the evidence contradicts a generic style assumption, the evidence wins.

# 2. What the Four Insider Reports Are

There are four master Insider Insights knowledge bases. The dominant Commercial Decision Approach selects the master pathway. Each master report then dynamically pulls the relevant secondary influence, six pillar-state blocks, constraint blocks, False Constraint, risk signals, conversation guidance and coaching guidance.

| **Report** | **Primary Approach** | **Core Internal Lens** | **Primary Development Risk** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Report A** | Future-Led | Possibility, future value, growth, expansion and what could be built. | Vision can outrun structure, completion or current commercial evidence. |
| **Report B** | Connection-Led | People, trust, relationships, conversation and human response. | Protecting the relationship can begin to replace commercial clarity or decision ownership. |
| **Report C** | Timing-Led | Readiness, context, practical flow, capacity and grounded judgement. | Waiting can continue after the conditions are already sufficient to move. |
| **Report D** | Evidence-Led | Proof, logic, numbers, process, risk and commercial certainty. | More evidence can stop improving the decision and start postponing it. |

The four reports must remain structurally comparable even where their section numbering differs in the current Word masters. For implementation, content families and Content IDs are authoritative. Section numbers are navigation aids for humans and should never be used as database keys.

# 3. Relationship to the Client-Facing Reports

Every test taker has one assessment record that can generate two different types of output. The client-facing report explains the result to the founder. The Insider Insights report interprets the same result for the authorised person who will sell to, advise, coach or guide that founder. The two outputs should agree on the underlying diagnosis but have different permission layers and different purposes.

| **Client-Facing Full Diagnostic** | **Insider Insights** |
| --- | --- |
| **Explains what is happening in the business.** | Explains how to work with the person behind the result. |
| **Shows six pillar scores, risk states and the Priority Fix Order.** | Uses those same states to alter selling, coaching and communication guidance. |
| **Explains the Primary and Secondary Constraint to the founder.** | Explains how the constraint may show up in conversation and what to listen for. |
| **Provides practical direction without internal persuasion guidance.** | Provides trust signals, objections, buying signals, resistance signals and conversation strategy. |
| **Does not expose hidden scoring, content IDs or behavioural hypotheses.** | May expose internal content IDs and build notes to authorised users or systems. |
| **Ends with client-appropriate next steps and disclaimer.** | Continues into coaching priorities, accountability and progress monitoring. |

The live systems should therefore keep the two knowledge bases in separate permission layers. Do not simply append Insider Insights to the client report and hide it with front-end styling. Treat the internal content as a separate authorised resource.

# 4. The Insider Engine in One Page

The engine should be understood as a sequence of evidence and interpretation rather than one combined score. The report becomes more intelligent because several layers are allowed to do different jobs.

| **Layer** | **Question It Answers** | **System Use** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Six Pillar Results** | What is actually happening in the business? | Primary commercial evidence. |
| **2. Constraint Engine** | Where is the highest leverage and what reinforces it? | Intervention sequence. |
| **3. Primary Approach** | How may this founder naturally enter or experience the pattern? | Behavioural hypothesis. |
| **4. Secondary Influence** | What modifies the primary behavioural pattern? | Contextual nuance. |
| **5. False Constraint + Risk Flags** | What may they misdiagnose or repeat? | Conversation and coaching clues. |
| **6. Live Conversation** | What is actually true for this person now? | Validation, adjustment and judgement. |

| **RENDERING FLOW** Assessment answers -> pillar raw scores -> pillar percentages and risk states -> overall readiness -> four approach percentages -> primary report -> secondary influence -> strongest pillar -> Constraint Engine -> False Constraint and supported risk flags -> six dynamic pillar blocks -> predictive selling and coaching blocks -> Quick Reference summaries -> final internal report. |

# 6. One Assessment, Four Insider Pathways

There is one 30-question assessment. Do not create four different questionnaires for the four approaches. The dominant Commercial Decision Approach selects the master internal report after the same assessment has produced the six commercial pillar scores.

| **Primary Approach** | **Master Insider Report** | **Allowed Secondary Influence Values** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | II-A | Connection-Led, Timing-Led, Evidence-Led, Balanced |
| **Connection-Led** | II-B | Future-Led, Timing-Led, Evidence-Led, Balanced |
| **Timing-Led** | II-C | Future-Led, Connection-Led, Evidence-Led, Balanced |
| **Evidence-Led** | II-D | Future-Led, Connection-Led, Timing-Led, Balanced |

The report letter belongs to the primary approach only. A Connection-Led founder with a strong Evidence-Led secondary still receives Report B, with the Connection-Led + Evidence-Led secondary block selected inside it. Do not create hybrid report files or choose the report from the secondary influence.

# 7. Assessment and Pillar Scoring

The assessment contains 30 behavioural scenario questions. Each of the six pillars receives five questions. Every answer carries a hidden pillar-readiness score of 0, 1, 2 or 3. The five values are added to create a raw pillar score from 0 to 15. The pillar percentage is the raw pillar score divided by 15 and multiplied by 100.

| **Raw Score** | **Displayed %** | **Risk State** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0** | 0% | High Risk |
| **1** | 6.7% | High Risk |
| **2** | 13.3% | High Risk |
| **3** | 20% | High Risk |
| **4** | 26.7% | High Risk |
| **5** | 33.3% | High Risk |
| **6** | 40% | Medium Risk |
| **7** | 46.7% | Medium Risk |
| **8** | 53.3% | Medium Risk |
| **9** | 60% | Medium Risk |
| **10** | 66.7% | Medium Risk |
| **11** | 73.3% | Low Risk |
| **12** | 80% | Low Risk |
| **13** | 86.7% | Low Risk |
| **14** | 93.3% | Low Risk |
| **15** | 100% | Low Risk |

The colour and internal signal are presentation layers. Green means Low Risk and should be interpreted internally as leverage. Amber means Medium Risk and should be interpreted as watch and strengthen. Red means High Risk and should be interpreted as investigate and intervene where commercially relevant. Store both the raw total and displayed percentage for auditability.

| **IMPORTANT** Never alter the pillar-readiness value because an answer is Future-Led, Connection-Led, Timing-Led or Evidence-Led. Readiness and behavioural approach are separate hidden fields. |

# 8. Overall Inevitable Standard Readiness

Overall Inevitable Standard Readiness is the arithmetic average of the six pillar percentages. Add the six percentages and divide by six, then display the result to one decimal place. Do not convert the overall result into Green, Amber or Red because the average can hide very different pillar risk patterns.

| **Overall %** | **Descriptor** | **Internal Use** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 to 39%** | Chance-Based | Large parts of the commercial system still depend on effort, circumstance or inconsistent behaviour. |
| **40 to 59%** | Inconsistent | Important structures exist, but performance varies enough to create meaningful leakage. |
| **60 to 79%** | Partly Structured | Much of the business is sound, with identifiable weaknesses that can become expensive as the business grows. |
| **80 to 100%** | Deliberate and Repeatable | The commercial system is strongly structured. Coaching should focus on leverage, accumulation and the remaining constraints rather than starting over. |

Overall readiness is context, not the intervention sequence. A founder with high overall readiness can still have one Red pillar that is commercially important. Conversely, a founder with low overall readiness may have one Green pillar that becomes valuable coaching leverage.

# 9. Commercial Decision Approach Scoring

The current implementation uses 18 dual-coded behavioural questions, three within each pillar. A dual-coded answer carries two separate hidden values: the normal 0/1/2/3 readiness value for its pillar and a separate signal for Commercial Decision Approach. The two values must remain independent in the data model and in the scoring code.

Aggregate the behavioural signals into four totals, then normalise them to percentages that sum to 100%. Store all four percentages. The largest percentage selects the primary approach and therefore the master Insider Insights report. The second-highest percentage informs secondary influence unless the configured rule selects Balanced.

The exact answer-to-approach signal weights belong in the final question-scoring specification and should be stored as versioned configuration. Do not reverse-engineer them from the report copy and do not hard-code them inside the rendering layer.

# 10. Primary and Secondary Influence Selection

The primary approach is the highest normalised approach percentage. The secondary influence is the next most meaningful approach and modifies the primary profile. It does not create a new score, overwrite pillar evidence or change the report letter.

If the primary percentages are exactly tied, use the versioned deterministic tie rule. The current fallback is to select the approach with the greater number of direct behavioural signals. If that also ties, use fixed A, B, C, D order and create an internal tie flag for QA. For secondary influence, use Balanced where second place is exactly tied or where the configured confidence threshold says no single secondary is sufficiently distinct.

| **SECONDARY INFLUENCE RULE** Render exactly one secondary influence block. Do not show all possible secondary variants and do not infer a secondary from pillar scores. |

# 11. Commercial Decision Map

The Commercial Decision Map can be retained in the internal snapshot if useful to advisers. The vertical axis is Commercial Risk Appetite, with Future-Led at the top and Timing-Led at the bottom. The horizontal axis is Decision Basis, with Evidence & Proof on the left and People & Trust on the right.

| **Coordinate** | **Formula** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Horizontal** | Connection-Led % minus Evidence-Led % | Positive values move right towards People & Trust. Negative values move left towards Evidence & Proof. |
| **Vertical** | Future-Led % minus Timing-Led % | Positive values move upward towards greater willingness to move while uncertainty remains. Negative values move downward towards greater readiness and timing preference. |

Do not describe the vertical axis as investment risk and do not describe the horizontal axis as facts versus feelings. These are commercial decision-behaviour dimensions, not investment suitability, psychological diagnosis or value judgements.

# 12. Dynamic Evidence Hierarchy

The internal report must use a hierarchy that prevents style stereotypes from overpowering actual results. The six pillar scores come first. The Constraint Engine comes second. The primary and secondary approaches explain how the pattern may be operating. Risk flags add answer-level nuance. The live conversation then validates the interpretation.

| **Priority** | **Evidence** | **Instruction** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Six pillar percentages and risk states | Treat as the strongest direct evidence of what is happening. |
| **2** | Primary and Secondary Constraint | Use to determine intervention sequence and conversation objective. |
| **3** | Strongest pillar / Green leverage | Use to show what already works and support the rebuild. |
| **4** | Primary Commercial Decision Approach | Use as behavioural lens, not as proof of a weakness. |
| **5** | Secondary influence | Use to modify tone, trust needs and decision pattern. |
| **6** | False Constraint and risk flags | Use only when supported by the mapped answers. |
| **7** | Live conversation | Validate, refine or reject hypotheses before acting on them. |

| **EXAMPLE** An Evidence-Led founder with Green Decision should not receive generic language that assumes chronic over-analysis. The Green Decision evidence tells the adviser that proof and logic are currently being managed well enough in that pillar. The same founder with Red Decision can receive a much stronger hypothesis around a moving proof threshold and delayed commitment. |
| --- |

# 13. Green, Amber and Red as Active Modifiers

Green, Amber and Red are not decorative score colours in Insider Insights. They actively modify how the predictive guidance is written and rendered. This is one of the most important differences between a useful internal report and a generic personality profile.

| **State** | **Internal Meaning** | **How It Changes the Report** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Green / Low Risk** | LEVERAGE | Use the approach-specific strength in this pillar as evidence of what already works. Avoid presenting the normal style risk as though it is established fact. |
| **Amber / Medium Risk** | WATCH + STRENGTHEN | Describe the approach-specific risk as inconsistent or conditional. Show what can destabilise the rebuild and what the adviser should monitor. |
| **Red / High Risk** | INVESTIGATE + INTERVENE | The approach-specific mechanism becomes a stronger hypothesis. Provide deeper listening cues, challenge guidance, selling implications and coaching implications, especially if the pillar is Primary or Secondary. |

The actual state must be used throughout the report, including How They Think, How They Decide, How They Buy, objections, trust guidance, coaching style and Quick Reference summaries. Do not generate the deep pillar block correctly and then leave the top-of-report predictive summary generic.

# 14. Dynamic Content Families and Coverage

Each master report contains fixed content plus dynamic families. The live report should select one relevant item from each required family rather than displaying the full knowledge base. Across all four reports, the dynamic library is intentionally large because the same commercial pillar can behave differently under different decision approaches.

| **Dynamic Family** | **Per Report** | **Across Four Reports** | **Selection Rule** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Secondary influence** | 4 | 16 | Render exactly one. |
| **Pillar state blocks** | 18 | 72 | Render one state for each of six pillars. |
| **Strongest pillar** | 6 | 24 | Render the highest pillar result, with deterministic tie fallback. |
| **Primary Constraint** | 6 | 24 | Render exactly one. |
| **Directional Primary + Secondary pairs** | 30 | 120 | Render exactly one ordered pair. |
| **False Constraints** | Core 4 + approach proposals | Varies | Render only where perceived-problem evidence and mapping support it. |
| **Risk signals** | Approach-specific | Varies | Render only answer-supported flags. |
| **Questions, objections, next-step framing** | 6 primary routes plus approach lens | 24+ families | Use Primary Constraint as the first selector, then modify with approach and supported states. |

The 72 pillar-state cells are particularly important. They represent six pillars multiplied by three risk states multiplied by four primary approaches. The 120 directional constraint-pair cells are equally important because Sales Primary + Decision Secondary is not the same as Decision Primary + Sales Secondary. Direction must remain part of the Content ID and selection rule.

# 17. How They Think

How They Think should remain a major standalone predictive section. It explains how the founder naturally processes commercial information, what she notices first, how she interprets opportunity and risk, how much detail she tends to want and what can happen under pressure. This is adapted from QSC’s predictive intelligence categories but grounded in the new Commercial Decision Approach and actual pillar evidence.

| **Approach** | **Baseline Thinking Pattern** |
| --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Starts with possibility, future value and strategic upside, then needs structure to make the future credible. |
| **Connection-Led** | Starts with people, trust, relationship implications and human response, then needs the commercial evidence and decision ownership brought back into view. |
| **Timing-Led** | Starts with context, readiness, capacity and practical flow, then needs clarity on which conditions are truly required to move. |
| **Evidence-Led** | Starts with proof, logic, numbers, process and risk, then needs clarity on when additional evidence has stopped improving the decision. |

The live text should be moderated by the pillar evidence. A Future-Led founder with Green Offer should not be told that she cannot translate vision into structure. An Evidence-Led founder with Green Sales should not be warned that she definitely over-explains. The deep pillar blocks are the source of truth for whether the style is currently an asset, an inconsistency or a risk in that area.

# 18. How They Decide

How They Decide is different from how they think. Thinking is how the founder processes the situation. Decision is what creates movement. The section should explain decision filters, speed, confidence requirements, hesitation points, what can reopen a decision and what a clean decision process looks like for this person.

Decision Green, Amber and Red must materially change the wording. Green means the natural approach is currently being managed well enough to support action. Amber means the threshold can become inconsistent. Red means the natural preference may be participating directly in decision delay or instability and deserves deeper validation in the conversation.

| **Approach** | **Typical Decision Question** | **Red-State Risk Hypothesis** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Is the future compelling enough? | New possibilities repeatedly compete with decisions already made. |
| **Connection-Led** | Does this work for the people involved? | Reassurance, approval or broad alignment become substitutes for decision ownership. |
| **Timing-Led** | Is this sensible now? | The readiness threshold keeps moving and "not yet" becomes the default. |
| **Evidence-Led** | What proves this makes sense? | The proof threshold moves and information gathering replaces commitment. |

# 19. How They Buy

How They Buy should be one of the strongest parts of the internal report because it translates the diagnostic into ethical sales preparation. It should explain what the founder is likely to evaluate, what creates confidence, what may look like an objection, what kind of information is useful and what the adviser needs to do to help the person reach a clean yes or no.

| **Approach** | **What Usually Creates Initial Engagement** | **What Must Follow** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | A meaningful future outcome, strategic possibility and what could become possible. | Sequence, scope, evidence and a credible path that prevents excitement from outrunning implementation. |
| **Connection-Led** | Feeling understood, trusting the person and seeing that the recommendation reflects the conversation. | A clear recommendation, transparent terms and a decision point that does not hide behind warmth. |
| **Timing-Led** | A practical path that respects current capacity and explains why the move makes sense now. | A precise definition of what must be true before movement and what can be built after starting. |
| **Evidence-Led** | A coherent commercial case, relevant proof, clear process and defined assumptions. | A decision threshold so additional evidence is gathered only when it could genuinely change the choice. |

Do not infer spending capacity, package size or willingness to invest from the Commercial Decision Approach alone. Style tells us how the founder may evaluate value. Offer fit must use diagnosed need, overall readiness, explicit business context, implementation capacity and the actual offer criteria.

# 20. Price and Value Interpretation

The old QSC concept of pricing tolerance should be adapted carefully. Insider Insights can explain how a person may evaluate price and value, but it should not predict a literal spending limit from behavioural style. The internal guidance should help the adviser understand what the investment needs to represent and what type of risk the founder may be trying to reduce.

| **Approach** | **Price / Value Lens** | **Adviser Focus** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Value is often understood through strategic upside and future possibility. | Connect the investment to the larger commercial outcome, then ground it in scope, sequence and evidence. |
| **Connection-Led** | Value is affected by trust in the person, relevance to the founder and the quality of the relationship. | Make the recommendation clearly personal to what was heard, without using relationship pressure. |
| **Timing-Led** | Value is judged partly by whether the investment is sensible and implementable now. | Show the practical pathway and separate real timing constraints from discomfort. |
| **Evidence-Led** | Value is judged through logic, proof, commercial reasoning and a defensible mechanism. | Make the commercial case explicit, then define what evidence is enough to decide. |

Identity, Sales and Revenue Model states should further modify this section. For example, a Red Identity result may create under-claiming or permission issues when the founder is evaluating investment in her own growth. A Red Revenue Model may make cash-flow or margin concerns objectively relevant rather than merely behavioural resistance.

# 21. Trust and Communication Intelligence

Trust guidance should combine primary approach, secondary influence and actual evidence. The adviser needs to know what increases confidence, what reduces it, what pace and level of detail to use, and how to communicate a difficult diagnosis so it can be heard without manipulating the founder.

| **Approach** | **Trust Builders** | **Trust Reducers** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Strategic fluency, bigger-picture understanding, a credible pathway, confidence and relevant proof. | Getting lost in detail before relevance, dismissing ambition, generic recommendations or exciting claims with no delivery path. |
| **Connection-Led** | Being heard, transparent intent, relevant examples, human understanding, respect for choice and consistent follow-through. | Cold generic selling, hidden agendas, dismissing people impact, artificial urgency or warmth without clarity. |
| **Timing-Led** | Practical understanding, calm confidence, respect for capacity, sequencing and a realistic implementation path. | Hype, overpromising, ignoring current reality or treating caution as weakness. |
| **Evidence-Led** | Competence, specificity, clear process, transparent assumptions, relevant data and commercial logic. | Vague claims, unsupported certainty, inconsistent logic, weak numbers or inspiration that avoids the commercial case. |

Secondary influence should refine rather than replace the primary pattern. Future + Evidence needs more commercial proof than Future + Connection. Connection + Timing may need stronger reassurance around pace and implementation. Evidence + Connection still needs logical clarity, but relationship credibility may determine whether the evidence is trusted.

# 22. Six-Pillar Coaching Intelligence

Every live Insider Insights report must render one dynamic coaching block for each of the six pillars. The block is selected by primary report and actual pillar state. This is where the 72 approach x pillar x state cells become operational.

| **Required Subsection Inside Each Pillar-State Block** | **Purpose** |
| --- | --- |
| **What This Result Tells You** | State the commercial meaning of the Green, Amber or Red result. |
| **How [Approach] Is Likely to Shape It** | Explain the approach-specific mechanism as a hypothesis. |
| **How This May Affect How They Think** | Connect the pillar result to information processing. |
| **How This May Affect How They Decide** | Connect the pillar result to decision behaviour. |
| **How This May Affect How They Buy** | Explain the likely effect when this founder is the buyer. |
| **How to Communicate This Result** | Tell the adviser how to explain the insight. |
| **What to Listen For** | Give phrases, patterns or behaviours that may validate the hypothesis. |
| **What to Challenge** | Identify the useful commercial challenge. |
| **What Not to Assume** | Prevent profiling stereotypes. |
| **Selling Implication** | Translate the result into ethical sales guidance. |
| **Coaching Implication** | Translate the result into post-sale coaching. |
| **Commercial Consequence to Track** | Identify measurable evidence of movement or ongoing leakage. |

A live report must render exactly six of these blocks, one for each pillar. Never render Green, Amber and Red alternatives together. Never substitute the client-facing pillar copy for the internal coaching block, because the internal block has a different purpose and permission level.

# 23. Strongest Pillar and Green Leverage

The strongest pillar is the highest pillar percentage. Render the matching Strongest Pillar block and use it as coaching leverage. The internal report should never become a catalogue of deficits. Green areas can demonstrate that the founder already possesses the commercial capability required to rebuild another area.

If pillar scores tie for strongest, use the fixed pillar order as the deterministic fallback unless a later versioned rule is approved. The fixed order is Identity, Positioning, Offer, Sales, Revenue Model, Decision.

| **GREEN LEVERAGE EXAMPLE** If Positioning is Green while Sales and Decision are Red, the adviser can use strong market clarity as evidence that more leads or another repositioning exercise may not be the first answer. The right people may already be reaching the business. The leverage sits in conversion and decision behaviour. |
| --- |

# 24. Constraint Engine

The Constraint Engine creates the strategic diagnosis that drives the internal report. Primary Constraint is the highest-leverage issue limiting the system now. It is not automatically the numerically lowest pillar. Secondary Constraint is the issue most likely to reinforce or recreate the Primary. False Constraint identifies a perceived problem the founder may be trying to solve too early. Priority Fix Order turns the diagnosis into sequence.

Identity and Decision require override capability because they can suppress otherwise strong structure. A founder can have a strong Offer but under-claim its value. A founder can have strong Positioning and still fail to execute the required commercial actions. The automated algorithm should therefore combine pillar evidence with configurable, versioned override rules rather than simply sorting scores.

| **IMPLEMENTATION STATUS** The reports already contain all six Primary Constraint blocks and all 30 directional Primary + Secondary pair blocks. If the final automated Constraint Engine rules are still being refined, keep them in a versioned configuration layer rather than embedding unchangeable logic in front-end code. |
| --- |

# 25. Primary + Secondary Constraint Loop

Render exactly one directional Primary + Secondary pair. Direction matters. Sales Primary + Decision Secondary means the immediate commercial leak is Sales and Decision is helping it repeat. Decision Primary + Sales Secondary means action and follow-through are the immediate issue, with Sales one of the areas being suppressed by that decision pattern.

The pair block should be used to explain the repeating loop, not to create two competing priorities. The Primary tells the adviser where to begin. The Secondary tells the adviser what may pull the founder back towards the old pattern if it remains unchanged.

| **Primary** | **Possible Secondary Values** | **Number of Directional Blocks** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Identity** | Positioning, Offer, Sales, Revenue Model, Decision | 5 |
| **Positioning** | Identity, Offer, Sales, Revenue Model, Decision | 5 |
| **Offer** | Identity, Positioning, Sales, Revenue Model, Decision | 5 |
| **Sales** | Identity, Positioning, Offer, Revenue Model, Decision | 5 |
| **Revenue Model** | Identity, Positioning, Offer, Sales, Decision | 5 |
| **Decision** | Identity, Positioning, Offer, Sales, Revenue Model | 5 |

# 26. False Constraint Intelligence

False Constraint is a perceived problem the founder may be trying to solve when the commercial evidence points somewhere else. It should create a useful reframe during the sales or coaching conversation, but only when the assessment contains the relevant perceived-problem signal. Do not assign a False Constraint merely because it fits the person’s approach.

| **Framework-Supported Core False Constraints** | **Typical First Reframe** |
| --- | --- |
| **I need more leads.** | If opportunities already exist but Sales or Decision is weak, more volume can increase the same leak. |
| **My price is too high.** | If strong-fit prospects engage but the founder discounts, over-explains or softens, Identity or Sales may need attention before changing the price. |
| **I need a new offer.** | If the Offer is clear but promotion, follow-up or decision rhythm is weak, rebuilding the Offer can delay the exposing commercial action. |
| **I need better systems.** | If the conversation is not reaching a decision, backend systems are unlikely to be the immediate leverage point. |

The four master reports also contain approach-specific proposed False Constraints, such as a bigger opportunity for Future-Led, a bigger community for Connection-Led, more time for Timing-Led and more data for Evidence-Led. Keep these proposed extensions inactive as formal diagnostic outputs until the final perceived-problem fields and mapping rules are approved. They may be used as internal hypotheses only where the assessment evidence supports them.

# 27. Dynamic Commercial Risk Signals

Commercial Risk Signals are answer-level supporting insights. They are not additional pillars and should never compete with the Constraint Engine. The report should render only flags supported by the underlying answer pattern. The approach changes the interpretation of the flag, but the approach alone must never create the flag.

| **Approach** | **Examples Already Present in the Master Knowledge Base** |
| --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Vision outrunning structure, Positioning churn, Offer expansion, inspiring without closing, scale before margin, optionality and decision drift, future promise risk, novelty as avoidance. |
| **Connection-Led** | Approval dependency, over-nurturing, soft ask, over-delivery and scope leakage, relationship-based pricing drift, consensus and reassurance delay, people-impact overreach. |
| **Timing-Led** | Under-claiming, waiting for demand, premium delay, buyer-readiness, slow margin leakage, founder adaptation, safe timing, rhythm risk, decision drift, capacity gate. |
| **Evidence-Led** | Proof threshold, technical Positioning, complexity, over-explanation, analysis-to-action, certainty, metric-hiding and proof-before-ask. |

Each risk flag should have at least three fields in the content layer: a stable code, an internal interpretation and an adviser response. If the flag influences the Quick Reference or objection guidance, those summaries should source from the same flag code rather than being independently authored.

# 28. Objections and What May Sit Underneath

The internal report should not use canned objection rebuttals. It should help the adviser interpret what a statement may mean and choose the next diagnostic question. The same sentence can have different meanings under different approaches and pillar states.

For example, "I need more time" may represent a real Timing-Led implementation constraint, a Decision delay, an unresolved commercial question or a polite no. "I need more information" may be a legitimate Evidence-Led request or a moving proof threshold. "I need to talk to my team" may be appropriate consultation for a Connection-Led founder or reassurance-seeking when Decision is weak. The adviser should validate rather than assume.

Use Primary Constraint as the first selector for the objection family. Modify the interpretation using relevant pillar states, secondary influence, False Constraint and risk flags. The report should explain what to ask next, not merely what to say back.

# 29. Predictive Conversation Strategy

The Insider Insights reports use Genene’s conversation application of The Inevitable Standard Method rather than manipulative pressure. The objective is a clean decision that serves the founder. The adviser should use the report to improve relevance, diagnosis and clarity, not to exploit behavioural tendencies.

| **Stage** | **Internal Purpose** |
| --- | --- |
| **Permission** | Create permission for a commercially honest conversation and establish what the founder wants from it. |
| **Reframe** | Help the founder see the difference between the perceived problem and the commercial evidence. |
| **Ownership** | Clarify what belongs to the founder and where behaviour or structure is keeping the pattern in place. |
| **Structure** | Explain the weak link, sequence and why the recommended order matters. |
| **Power Questions** | Use questions selected from Primary Constraint and approach to deepen recognition. |
| **Embodiment** | Help the founder experience what a stronger commercial standard would look like in a real situation. |
| **Result** | Reach a clear next decision, including a clean no where appropriate, rather than leaving an indefinite maybe. |

The Commercial Decision Approach changes the delivery of the method. Future-Led may need the future outcome established before detail. Connection-Led may need enough relational trust for direct challenge. Timing-Led may need the conditions for action made explicit. Evidence-Led may need the commercial logic and proof threshold made clear.

# 30. Pre-Call Preparation and Questions

The report should support a short pre-call preparation routine. The adviser should be able to review the Quick Reference, confirm the Primary and Secondary Constraint, identify Green leverage, note the likely False Constraint and risk flags, and select the questions most likely to validate the central hypothesis.

| **Pre-Call Check** | **Question for the Adviser** |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary Constraint** | What is the one commercial issue I need to understand more deeply? |
| **Secondary Constraint** | What may be recreating the Primary problem? |
| **Green leverage** | What existing strength can I use to avoid unnecessary rebuilding? |
| **False Constraint** | What problem does the founder appear likely to solve first, and what evidence would show it is secondary? |
| **Approach** | What type of framing, proof, trust or pacing is likely to help the insight land? |
| **Risk flags** | Which answer-level behaviours are worth validating rather than assuming? |
| **Next step** | What outcome would make this conversation useful even if the founder does not buy? |

The deep report should also provide question sets by Primary Constraint and one "question they may be avoiding" for each pillar. These are coaching and diagnostic prompts, not scripts that must be read verbatim.

# 31. Challenge Sequence

Each report includes guidance on what to challenge and what not to challenge too early. This is essential because a correct interpretation delivered at the wrong point in the conversation can reduce trust and make the insight harder to hear. The build should preserve the sequence guidance rather than extracting isolated challenge phrases into a generic script library.

The adviser should normally validate the founder’s stated concern, establish enough evidence for the commercial reframe, and then challenge the behaviour or assumption that is actually relevant. Where a risk flag is only a hypothesis, the language should remain investigative rather than declarative.

| **COACHING RULE** Challenge the pattern, not the person. Use the founder’s own answers and commercial evidence. A behavioural hypothesis becomes useful only when the conversation confirms that it is actually operating. |
| --- |

# 32. Buying and Resistance Signals

Buying Signals and Resistance Signals help the adviser read movement without pretending to predict the future with certainty. They should be described as likely signals, not guarantees. A signal should influence the next question, not replace the direct ask.

| **Approach** | **Examples of Buying Movement** | **Examples of Resistance or Drift** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Future-Led** | Begins speaking about the future as achievable, connects the recommendation to a larger objective, asks about implementation or next-stage possibilities. | Keeps opening unrelated possibilities, becomes excited but will not choose a next step, disengages when implementation becomes concrete. |
| **Connection-Led** | Uses collaborative language, asks about support and relationship, reflects trust in the adviser, begins discussing who else needs to be involved appropriately. | Stays warm but non-committal, seeks repeated reassurance, introduces more people to avoid ownership, protects harmony from a direct decision. |
| **Timing-Led** | Begins defining practical start conditions, asks how implementation fits capacity, identifies a realistic decision point. | Adds another condition after each one is met, repeatedly moves the start date, uses time without naming what new information it will create. |
| **Evidence-Led** | Begins testing implementation detail, narrows remaining questions, accepts assumptions and starts discussing the specific commercial case. | Keeps widening the evidence set, requests more proof without a decision purpose, introduces new comparisons after earlier concerns are resolved. |

# 33. Recommended Next Step and Offer Fit

The recommended next step should be selected from the actual diagnosed need and offer-routing rules, then positioned in language appropriate to the founder’s approach. Do not choose an offer from personality style alone. The primary approach changes how the recommendation is explained, not which package the founder can afford or what the business objectively needs.

Primary Constraint should be the main selector for the value proposition. Overall readiness, business context, implementation capacity and explicit commercial facts can then influence fit. The report may also use the Priority Fix Order to explain why one pathway should come before another.

| **OFFER-FIT BOUNDARY** Do not infer spending level, creditworthiness, financial suitability or willingness to invest from the Commercial Decision Approach. Insider Insights is behavioural and commercial guidance, not financial profiling. |
| --- |

# 34. Follow-Up Guidance

Follow-up should be approach-aware and evidence-aware. The report should tell the adviser what to reiterate, which uncertainty may need resolving, what additional proof or clarity could help, and what not to send. Follow-up should not become an automated pressure sequence simply because the report predicts a likely objection.

Where the founder has not decided, use the earlier conversation to determine what remains unresolved. For Timing-Led, clarify whether more time has a defined purpose. For Evidence-Led, clarify whether additional evidence could change the decision. For Connection-Led, keep the relationship warm without allowing the decision to disappear. For Future-Led, reconnect the recommendation to the strategic outcome and then bring it back to the agreed next step.

# 35. Post-Sale Coaching Priorities

Once the founder becomes a client, the same internal report becomes a coaching map. The coach should not discard the predictive selling layer and start again. The approach, pillar states, Primary and Secondary Constraint, Green leverage and risk flags continue to explain how the founder is likely to respond to the rebuild.

Use Priority 1 as the main coaching focus. Use Priority 2 to identify what may recreate the problem. Use Priority 3 only after the first two are moving unless the live evidence requires a change. Green pillars should be used to support confidence and transfer proven behaviour into the constrained area.

The book should remain the intervention library. Identity coaching should return to value, visibility, asking and the Identity Gap. Offer coaching should return to productisation, outcome, ladder, scope and margin. Sales coaching should return to clean decisions and the conversation method. Decision coaching should return to rhythm, pre-decided revenue actions and follow-through. Revenue Model coaching should return to margin, retained value and the Revenue-To-Freedom chain.

# 36. Accountability and Progress Signals

Accountability should be behavioural and commercially observable. The coach should avoid measuring progress only through how confident or clear the founder says she feels. Each pillar has observable evidence of movement.

| **Pillar** | **Examples of Progress Evidence** |
| --- | --- |
| **Identity** | Price stated without unnecessary softening, clearer asks, stronger boundaries, less discounting, more visible authority. |
| **Positioning** | Clearer audience and problem signal, stronger lead fit, less message churn, shorter explanation required. |
| **Offer** | Defined scope, stronger ladder, cleaner premium pathway, improved margin, less bespoke rebuilding. |
| **Sales** | More direct recommendations, consistent follow-up, more closed loops, improved conversion and shorter unresolved cycles. |
| **Revenue Model** | Improved margin, stronger repeat revenue, healthier owner value, lower dependency and clearer client progression. |
| **Decision** | Fewer open loops, clearer deadlines, protected revenue rhythm, less reopening, faster implementation of already-decided work. |

The approach changes how accountability is delivered. Future-Led often benefits from fewer active priorities and visible completion. Connection-Led may need boundaries and permission to tolerate relational discomfort. Timing-Led benefits from measurable readiness thresholds and dates. Evidence-Led benefits from explicit decision criteria and limits on additional analysis.

# 37. Green and Red Flags for the Adviser

The reports include adviser-level Green Flags and Red Flags. These are not pillar colours. They are call-management and coaching signals that help the adviser decide whether to proceed, slow down, ask for more evidence, challenge a pattern, change the intervention or refer the founder to another professional where appropriate.

Green Flags might include clear ownership, the ability to describe the commercial problem accurately, willingness to act on evidence, realistic expectations, strong fit between need and recommended pathway, and an existing Green pillar that can support the rebuild. Red Flags might include unsupported urgency, refusal to engage with the actual constraint, material financial or legal issues outside scope, expectations of guaranteed outcomes, or a mismatch between the recommended intervention and the founder’s actual needs.

# 42. Permission Layer and Confidentiality

Insider Insights is confidential internal material. It contains behavioural hypotheses, trust and resistance signals, objection interpretations, adviser responses, pre-call guidance, coaching strategy and implementation logic. These should not appear automatically in the client-facing report or be exposed through public report URLs.

Authorised users may include Genene Wilson, approved advisers, coaches, consultants, implementation personnel and licensed partners according to contractual permissions. The platform should support role-based access, and rendered report URLs should respect that permission layer.

| **CLIENT BOUNDARY** The founder can receive the commercial diagnosis, their Commercial Decision Approach and practical guidance. The insider can receive hypotheses about how to communicate, sell, challenge, coach and interpret resistance. Keep those layers distinct unless a future product release explicitly approves a client-facing version of an internal insight. |

# 43. Coach and Adviser Operating Guide

The internal report is a preparation and coaching aid, not a substitute for judgement. Advisers should read the Quick Reference before the call, use the deeper sections where relevant, and validate the hypotheses through conversation. They should not read the report aloud to the founder or announce inferred traits as fixed facts.

| **Before the Call** | **During the Call** | **After the Call** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Review Primary/Secondary Constraint, Green leverage, False Constraint, relevant risk flags and conversation objective.** | Use the approach to improve communication, then test the commercial hypothesis through questions. Keep the weak link central. | Record what was validated, what changed, the next decision, and any content/risk interpretation that proved inaccurate. |
| **Select 3 to 5 useful questions rather than trying to use every prompt.** | Use a clean decision process. Do not manufacture urgency or use the profile to corner the founder. | If the founder becomes a client, convert the same report into coaching priorities and measurable progress signals. |
| **Know what not to challenge too early.** | Use Green pillars as evidence of existing capability and avoid unnecessary rebuilding. | Update CRM notes separately from the immutable assessment result. Do not rewrite the original score because the conversation added nuance. |

If the live conversation contradicts the behavioural hypothesis, the coach should trust the person and the evidence from the conversation. The report is designed to improve preparation, not to prove itself right. Capture repeated contradictions for model review because they may indicate copy, mapping or scoring improvements.

# 44. Ethical and Professional Boundaries

Insider Insights should help the adviser make a conversation more relevant, respectful and commercially clear. It must not be used for coercion, hidden manipulation or unsupported claims about psychology. The Commercial Decision Approach is a commercial behavioural framework, not a clinical diagnosis or fixed personality type.

The report can discuss Revenue, Profit, Personal Wealth and Freedom as commercial concepts, but it is not personal financial, investment, tax, legal, accounting or credit advice. Do not use the profile to infer financial capacity, investment suitability, creditworthiness or personal wealth. Where the conversation moves into regulated or professional advice, refer the founder to an appropriately licensed or qualified practitioner.

No part of the report should imply guaranteed revenue, profit, wealth, business, coaching or investment outcomes. Buying and resistance signals are hypotheses. Objections are interpretations to investigate. Recommended next steps require real fit and informed choice.

## Open Implementation Items to Lock Before Production

The current report architecture is complete enough to build, but several rules should remain configurable until formally signed off. The exact answer-to-approach weights for the 18 dual-coded behavioural questions belong in the final scoring specification. The exact confidence threshold for selecting a named secondary influence versus Balanced should also be versioned. The automated Constraint Engine should remain configurable until its override logic has been validated against real cases.

The approach-specific False Constraint extensions are useful internal hypotheses but should not become formal diagnostic outputs until the perceived-problem fields and mapping rules are approved. The same principle applies to risk flags: every flag needs a documented answer-level trigger, not an inference from approach alone.

For content storage, normalise item-level Content IDs across A, B, C and D, particularly False Constraints and risk signals. The Word masters are authoritative content sources, but the production content service should use a consistent schema even where the current documents group some items differently.
